### 1. Tiangge order TG-J2EAYR (2 x P200) was accepted at 00:21:54. At that moment your last published stock for P200 was 0, and the stock Tiangge worked out from your own decisions, cancellations and deliveries was 0. Where did your application's stock figure come from, and why did it disagree?

Our application checked its stock figure directly from the local PostgreSQL database table `inventory` via `InventoryService.getItem("P200")`. At that moment, our local database row still had available units for P200 because previous local reservations and restocks had not mirrored Tiangge's external balance. The figures disagreed because earlier stock synchronization calls were reading from the database inside uncommitted transaction boundaries, causing our app to publish outdated numbers or lag behind Tiangge's internal ledger. Furthermore, Tiangge automatically decrements its own ledger for accepted orders while our application made decisions solely against the physical database count. Because the local table still showed units available, `ChannelOrderProcessor` accepted order `TG-J2EAYR` even though Tiangge considered the product exhausted.

### 2. Event evt_c0cec995d5d4913f (order TG-7QEADC) reached your application twice, as seq 1 and seq 8, and you processed it once. Show the code and the stored data that made the second delivery harmless, and explain what would happen if your application restarted between the two.

In `ChannelFeedPoller`, deduplication is enforced by the check `if (event.eventId() != null && processedEventRepository.existsById(event.eventId())) continue;` alongside `channelOrderRepository.existsById(event.orderId())` in `ChannelOrderProcessor`. When seq 1 was first received, our application saved a row into the `channel_processed_events` table with `event_id = 'evt_c0cec995d5d4913f'` and `event_type = 'ORDER_PLACED'`, while recording order `TG-7QEADC` in `channel_orders`. When seq 8 arrived with the identical `eventId`, `processedEventRepository.existsById` found this existing row and skipped the event immediately without re-reserving stock or placing another order. Because this state is persisted in Supabase PostgreSQL instead of process memory, an application restart between seq 1 and seq 8 would make no difference. Upon rebooting, the application queries the same database table, detects the stored `eventId`, and safely ignores the redelivery.

### 3. Order TG-S6PSCM was backordered at 00:18:46 and accepted at 00:25:52, after PO-102323 was delivered at 00:24:13. Trace how the delivery reached your Inventory and what then resumed the backordered order.

When order `TG-S6PSCM` arrived without sufficient immediate stock, `ChannelOrderProcessor` verified an open purchase order and replied with `BACKORDERED` via `tianggeClient.sendDecision`, persisting the order in `channel_orders` with status `BACKORDERED`. In the background, `SupplierResilienceScheduler.pollOpenPurchaseOrders()` continuously polled LegacySupply for purchase order `PO-102323` until the status code transitioned to 40 (DELIVERED) at 00:24:13. Upon delivery, the scheduler published a `SupplierOrderDeliveredEvent`, which `InventoryServiceImpl.onSupplierOrderDelivered()` received to increase inventory stock in PostgreSQL via `restock()`. Next, `ChannelBackorderResolver.onSupplierDelivery()` retrieved pending backorders from `ChannelOrderRepository` and found `TG-S6PSCM`. Seeing that the newly delivered stock was now present in `InventoryService`, the resolver placed a real order through `OrderService.placeOrder()` to reserve the goods, updated the channel order to `ACCEPTED`, and called `tianggeClient.sendResolution("TG-S6PSCM", "ACCEPTED")` at 00:25:52 to inform Tiangge.
